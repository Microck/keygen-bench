# Frequently asked questions

## Were keygen tunes “coded”?

Usually they were sequenced in a tracker rather than written note-by-note in a programming language. Code still handled replay, executable integration, and procedural synthesizers.

## Was FastTracker II the program?

Sometimes. XM is FastTracker II’s native format, but XM can also be written or resaved by MilkyTracker, ModPlug/OpenMPT, converters, and other tools.

## Was ProTracker the program?

For classic Amiga MOD work, often plausibly. A later Windows keygen containing MOD does not prove ProTracker or Amiga authorship.

## Was Impulse Tracker used?

IT is Impulse Tracker’s native format, but other trackers can write IT too.

## Was ModPlug Tracker/OpenMPT used?

It is technically capable of creating the relevant formats. Per-song attribution still needs metadata, edit history, source, or reliable documentation.

## Was the music MIDI?

Sometimes. MIDI stores note/control events and requires a synthesizer or sound bank. XM/MOD/IT/S3M generally contain their own sample material.

## Was it all 8-bit?

No. Classic MOD commonly uses 8-bit PCM, while XM supports 8- and 16-bit samples and IT supports richer sample/instrument structures.

## Did the keygen generate the music algorithmically?

Usually not when the embedded data is a conventional XM, MOD, S3M, or IT module. V2M and 4klang are genuine procedural-synthesis routes.

## Why were the files small?

Because modules reuse samples, patterns, instruments, effects, and loops instead of storing every second as a rendered recording.

## Why did they loop?

The hosting window could remain open indefinitely, so a seamless loop was more practical than silence or a long recording.

## How were chords made with few channels?

Possible methods included arpeggio effects, sampled chords, rapid note alternation, or several notes across several channels.

## How were reverb and delay made cheaply?

Common methods duplicated channels, delayed events, lowered volume, and changed panning. Some effects were also resampled into short reusable samples.

## Did they use hardware synths?

Possibly upstream, but a finished module usually does not preserve that provenance.

## Can a module’s tracker field identify the software?

It can identify the writer recorded in that saved file. It may be a converter or later editor rather than the original composition program.

## Can the playback library be identified from the module?

No. Runtime-player identity is external to the module and requires executable/source evidence.

## Were uFMOD and MiniFMOD composition tools?

No. They are XM replay libraries.

## Is 4klang a tracker?

It is better described as a modular software-synth system with a VSTi authoring route and compact runtime export.

## Is every archived tune legally reusable?

No. Public availability is not a license.
