# Creative brief: original keygen tune

Create an original keygen/scene tune as a genuinely editable XM, with a lossless WAV preview. Produce music only.

Choose the musical identity, tempo, harmony, rhythm, sound palette, length and arrangement yourself. Use the tracker tradition as a vocabulary, not a formula or a requirement to imitate an existing piece. Do not adopt the smoke-test fixture as a musical template.

Compose with notes, patterns, instruments, samples and tracker effects. Create and shape original sound material as needed, including through project-local synthesis code. Do not disguise a complete prerecorded song as one sample.

Inspect the available tools before composing. Keep the tracker session alive across edits. Save versioned checkpoints. When a required creative operation is missing, either implement and test a project-local extension within the granted permissions or state the limitation. Do not pretend the existing bridge exposes every tracker feature.

Render and evaluate the actual audio throughout development. Use technical checks and, where a real audio-input capability is available, listening-based criticism. Evaluate the complete tune, important transitions, and repeated playback of its intended loop. Compare revisions for identifiable improvements rather than automatically favoring louder or busier output. Preserve the better version.

Make ordinary creative decisions independently. Stay within the configured time, storage and tool budget, keeping a complete playable version. Use original or appropriately licensed source material.

Deliver the XM, WAV preview, any source synthesis/editing scripts needed to reproduce it, loop/restart information, and a brief test record. Distinguish completed tests from unavailable checks. Rendering a file does not mean you heard it; explicitly say when no audio-based review took place.
